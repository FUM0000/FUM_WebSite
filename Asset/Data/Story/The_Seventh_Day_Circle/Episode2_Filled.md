# Episode 2 "Filled"

In the morning, a circle. —Or so it should have been.

I had been thinking about last night's laughter. I stand in front of the wall and my hand stops. The circle runs longer than usual. It should not, I tell myself. My habit had loosened, just a little. —Maybe it is nothing. The sphere said nothing. Only, my circle stopped slightly closer to the start than the usual eight-tenths.

The automatic cooker brought breakfast at the set hour, as always: the same things. A hamburger. Broccoli on the side. Soup. All the same taste, all uniformly soft, all "delicious." But sometimes I cannot decide whether any of it deserves to be called a taste. Perfectly delicious things have no memory. —Shizuku said as much.

"The sky outside is unsavory. Because it has taste."

Yesterday, at the hot spring, that was what she said.

"The sky of Ringa is beautiful. Blue, clear forever. But it is unsavory, that sky. —Outside, the sky has rain falling in it. In winter the wind blows. Wood rots, there is the smell of earth, things dry in the sun. Everything is eaten by the years, rots, and still it grows again. It has taste only then."

I did not really understand. But her words stayed with me, strangely.

The days go on filling me up.

And what is wrong with that, Ringa says. Food is sufficient, safety is total, sorrow is erased for you. Even the places to cry and the places to rejoice are administered. Sad memories are harvested before they can become memory, so no one grieves. No one gets angry. No one cries. —I am filled. Smooth as a plaster patch, peaceful. I thought that too, at first.

At noon, Hako was making a fuss. Hako, Ringa's administrator, a man in hard clothes like cardboard, in charge of the circle routine. He pointed one finger at the sky, his voice shaking.

"I saw it! I saw a full circle!"

Hako says that last night, a full circle had been drawn on the back wall of the Inner Ring. A perfectly round thing, glowing softly.

"That is the sign before Rindō! Someone is about to be swallowed by the sphere!"

At Hako's voice, residents gathered and peered into the back wall. And indeed there was something circle-shaped. Milky white light, the ring entirely closed.

—Except that the line sat twenty centimeters above the floor.

"Hako, sir. That's a mark left by Tapi when he fell over."

In front of the wall lay a small spherical robot, upside down, kicking its little legs. A dull drone with legs — a sphere that flies all over Ringa whistling. —A favorite of Gurau's, that sphere at the center.

"That sphere," Shizuku told me later, "made that one too well."

"Why does Gurau do such things?"

"Ah. That sphere, you see, Maru—"

Shizuku narrowed her eyes across the steam.

"Because you die, there is love."

—Because I die?

I have never seen a human being die in Ringa. Everyone does Rindō. Rises. Graduates. —In this town, death does not exist.

Night. I get into bed. The fragments of my dreams fade. The sphere eats eight-tenths.

—And then, in a corner of my dream, there was a sharp sound. Like a whistle. Like noise. Like laughter.

"...Haru?"

No answer. But that night, my dream stayed a little longer than it usually did.

I am filled. I keep filling up. —And yet something seemed to want to be filled.
