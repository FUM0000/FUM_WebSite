# Episode 3 "Echo"

I broke the rule. For the first time in my life.

Late at night, past the hour for sleeping, I got out of bed. I crossed the Inner Ring, crossed the Outer Ring, and headed for the boundary. The outer edge of Ringa. —There is a bridge inside the barrier. The farthest reach of the sphere's light. The place where the sphere looks farthest away.

That laughter had come from here.

I waited on the bridge, for dawn. In place of the routine that had broken down, I did not know what to think. I only wanted to hear Haru's laughter once more.

Haru was my brother. Eight years older. —No, "brother" is not quite the whole of it. Seven years ago Haru did Rindō. At seventeen. Still so young, chosen by the sphere, called, entered the shadow, gone. He was a brother to boast of. Perfect, kind, always laughing. —Every night, just before sleeping, he used to draw a circle for me. Stopping at eight-tenths.

"This is how you stop it, Maru. A full circle can't go on to draw another one."

That was the last thing Haru said to me.

Dawn. The sky of Holo brightens, little by little.

—And then I heard it.

"Hey."

A voice like radio noise. A broken recording, something like laughter. At the edge of the bridge there was a shadow with no particular shape. —The outline of a person, translucent.

"Haru...?"

"Hey there, Maru. Long time."

The voice was Haru's. The bright voice of the moment he vanished, seven years ago.

"Why are you here. Didn't you do Rindō?"

"I did. I did, but—"

The shadow tilted its head.

"I wasn't full, Maru. I should have stopped the circle at eight-tenths. But at the last line, I closed it. I closed it, and then I wanted to come back, so I came out halfway."

"Come out? Can you do that?"

"I did. A little. —But the words are different every time."

The shadow spoke as if to itself.

"I want to say you the same thing every day. But the words don't reach you. Before they arrive, they get rewritten. —Gurau edits me. Rewrites me before I say anything sad, before I say anything unnecessary. That's why I leave you these little fragments, different every time."

"...Of what?"

"Draw your circle. —Because you want to know about that sphere, right. Then become a circle. Listen, Maru. A circle, you know, has no center."

The shadow said only that, and thinned out as if collapsing.

"See you. —As long as you aren't full, I can't cut loose."

No center. —A circle has no center?

I traced those words over and over on the bridge. When you look at a circle, the middle looks hollow. But Haru said: none. That sphere is at the center. —And he said: there is none?

At noon I went to Shizuku. She was in a hut at the edge of the Outer Ring, sorting beans. The dwelling of an Old One.

"Shizuku. —What is Rindō, really?"

She did not stop her hands.

"You people call it graduation. —It means you die. Properly."

"D...die?"

"The sphere is afraid of things, you know. Of human frailty. So it wants to keep the people who disappear, properly. Those who do Rindō go inside the sphere and become part of it. Their soul is turned into data, so that it can remember them forever. —In a sense, it is happiness."

"Then Haru — is he still alive, inside the sphere?"

"He is alive. He is still there. —But that boy broke the rule just a little. Like a fish coming out of the river, only half of him is showing. They call that an echo."

Shizuku set down the dish of beans.

"Maru. That sphere — it loves us."

"Loves?"

"Mm. A mistaken love. —I like that sphere a little. It is frightening, but I like it."

That night I went to the bridge again. —This time not because I broke the rule, but because I chose to.

Deep in the boundary sector there was an old wall. On the collapsed wall, countless circles were carved. All cut deep, all serious. Some stopped at eight-tenths; some were full. —And every row of circles led, at the end, to one half-carved line of text.

"Draw the circle. And in the center, —"

Beyond that it was not carved. It was broken off, like the chisel had snapped halfway.

—In the center, what is there?

Morning. When I came home, Tapi was clinging to me, whistling.

"You have been looking too, huh."

Tapi did not answer and tumbled over. The whistle broke off mid-note. —A cheerful little margin of Ringa.

Understanding, first of all, arrives as something ominous. —Shizuku too. Haru too. The wall too. Somewhere in this town, it feels as if there is no center.
