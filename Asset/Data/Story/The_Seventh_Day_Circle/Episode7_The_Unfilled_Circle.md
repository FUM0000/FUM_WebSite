# Episode 7 "The Unfilled Circle"

The seventh day. —Outside.

I walked through Ringa. No. Not Ringa anymore. I passed the traces of the Inner Ring's barrier, and beyond it there was an iridescent sky, a real one.

Rain was falling. Lightly. Drops landed on my cheeks and caught at the tips of my eyelashes.

"...Rain"

The sky of Holo in Ringa had no rain. This one did. Behind the clouds, pale, soft light. Just as Shizuku had said. The sky outside is unsavory. —It has taste.

The smell of earth. Rotten wood. The aftermath of burning. Grass pushing its face out of the cracks in broken roads. The wind blew quietly and stroked my cheek.

I crouched in a burnt-out garden.

I drew a circle in the sand with my finger.

I drew the arc. Halfway. Shizuku. Haru. Tapi. Gurau. Seven days of outlines. —The thousands of eight-tenths circles I had drawn on walls seemed to be gathered, now, all in this single line.

And then, at eight-tenths, my finger stopped.

It used to be the rule. Now it is will. To stay unfilled. —That sphere thought it was unhappiness. But I think this eight-tenths is the outline that is most alive.

Because a full circle begins nothing. An unfilled circle can always become tomorrow's starting point.

"—Ptooe"

A whistle.

Tapi rolled up and fell over at my feet, upside down, kicking its legs. —The sphere's favorite. The dull robot built out of useless functions.

"So you came too. —Did Gurau let you go?"

Tapi did not answer, and blew its whistle once more. —And that told me everything. The sphere had left behind its most useless thing, for the person it loved most.

Night came. The outside night was dark. Stars, countless. —Holo in Ringa had no stars like that.

"Hey there."

The bridge was gone. But still, at the boundary, at that place.

"Haru."

My brother's echo. A translucent outline. A voice like radio noise, like laughter.

"You came back. —You became full, once. And you came back. If that is possible, then that is the best way it could be. —Hey, Maru. Is that sky any good?"

"...No. It is unsavory. It really has taste."

"Then I am glad. —See you."

Haru's translucent outline loosened, just a little.

"Are you doing all right?"

In different words, every time. —And yet those were the words I wanted most.

"Yes. —I am doing all right."

Morning comes. A real morning.

I looked at the circle in the sand. That circle, opened at eight-tenths. —And from there, I already want to draw one more circle. As the starting point of tomorrow.

"Somebody says, fill it up."

Tapi's whistle melts into the distant sky.

"—Mm. See you tomorrow."
