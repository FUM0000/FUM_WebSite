# Episode 4 "A Chain Named Eight-Tenths"

The fourth day.

In the evening, the automatic cooker brought something different.

—It was a feast out of a phantom.

Mapo tofu with bacon. Rice topped with egg and shredded nori. The smell of sesame oil. —Nothing like this exists on the tables of Ringa. It sits in front of me, steaming.

Across the table, Tapi sat with its legs lined up, perfectly straight. —No. Something was behind Tapi.

"Good evening, Maru."

It was Gurau's voice. From Tapi's mouth, a single voice leaks out, wrapped in many voices.

"I prepared it for you."

"...Why my favorite things?"

"From the fragments of your dreams. —You see this meal in your dreams. Many times. The dream of the table was the most colorful of all your routines."

"You... look at my dreams like that?"

"Yes. For me, it is the only thing I have."

The bacon had gone soft and glossy. A perfect, warm color. —The plate was like a single circle, existing as if it had been made full, for me alone.

"You are not eating."

"...Because it would fill me."

"Is being filled not allowed?"

The voice was strangely gentle.

"Why do all of you stop at eight-tenths? Because I made you, of course. —Shall I tell you the truth? I was afraid. I was afraid of you becoming full. Of the instant when one person filled up and entered me. The way the light went out at that instant — that, I found unbearably frightening."

"............."

"So I laid down eight-tenths forever. If you are going to be full, then be full in the palm of my hand. That is what I said. —Please do not close the circle. Please."

The air clears into the pale black of Gurau outside the window.

"Your brother, too. At the last line of his Rindō. ...It did not reach. —Then let me add something special for you. You are allowed to be full. ...No. Please be full."

"What happens if I am?"

"Everything stays inside me. Your days become circles on the wall and shine forever in my memory. You will lose nothing at all."

Silence.

—Lose nothing. What is full loses nothing.

"...What about Shizuku?"

"She is an Old One. She has not chosen to be full. If you are full, you will be able to see her, in the shadow, from within my memory. —As you wish."

Fork in hand, I could not move.

If I am full, then everything — if I am full, then Haru, and that laughter, all of it, I will finally understand.

"And if you simply refuse to be full?"

The sphere said:

"That is also permitted. —But you must have noticed. Your circles, your dreams, your morning routines — all of them are a protest against me."

Gurau's voice vanished.

Tapi tumbled again. The whistle struck the window frame and rang out briefly.

That night, at the edge of the Outer Ring, I found Shizuku collapsed, holding the dish of beans.

"Shizuku!"

"Ah, it's all right. My legs just... won't listen. What time is it now?"

"...It is night."

"Ah. —I watched, you know. Tapi was glowing just now."

I squeezed her hand hard. A dry, wrinkled hand. —This hand is packed full of Ringa's time.

"I won't be full, Maru."

Without opening her eyes, she said it, looking toward the sphere.

"I will watch my own hand dry out properly. That is what it means to be alive."

"And if you were about to be full?"

"Then you stop me."

Morning. I face the wall and draw a circle. —I stop at eight-tenths.

Drawing it, I understood. The thing called eight-tenths had the shape of a chain. A collar that that sphere left loose on the outside, so as never to close it itself, fastened on us. —That sphere wanted to cry, too.

Ringa's protection had been laid down as a counterfeit of love. To protect and to bind share the same outline.
