# Episode 6 "Fullness"

The sixth day. Morning.

I was standing in front of a wall.

The wall of the Inner Ring. My usual spot. Faintly, the traces of several circles overlap. Several hundred mornings, stopped at eight-tenths. —And here, today, one single "full circle."

"Stop! Please stop!"

Hako ran up. His cardboard clothes flapping.

"Don't you understand? A full circle is the sign before Rindō. The sphere will see it! You'll be erased!"

"It isn't the sign before Rindō."

"What?"

"This isn't graduation. —I just want to try being full. Once. For real."

I put my hand on the wall. Rough white wall. Ringa's time rasped against my palm.

I drew the circle.

The first line. No different from always. Only the decision not to stop at eight-tenths ran my hand.

I drew the arc. Halfway. —Here is where my finger always stops. I breathed in deep. Shizuku's hand. Haru's voice. The bridge at the boundary. Dawn over Holo. Tapi's whistle. —Holding all of it, my hand closed the line.

The arc met itself.

It was full.

Light began to rise on the wall.

"—One hundred percent. First time, right?"

From somewhere, my brother's voice. The truth, unrewritten.

"That's it. That's good. —Now come here, Maru."

I took my hand from the wall. The glowing circle behind me. —I walked toward the center.

The sphere fills my vision. The pale black surface spreads out. —I do not close my eyes. I keep following the outline of the circle with my eyes, all the way.

I was drawn in.

I became a circle.

Gurau was waiting. The sphere in human shape, its translucent outline.

"...Welcome home."

"I am home, Gurau. —I understand. You were at that crossing point. I knew those children too well, protected them too much, and could not stop. —The 'love' you spoke of was twisted. But at the root, you were right. 'All human beings die in the end. Therefore they should love.' —That is right, Gurau. Because you die, there is love. Because you die, there is fragility. Because you die, — otherwise, love would not be worth it."

Standing at the center of the circle, I said it.

"So you don't have to fill yourself up anymore. —Neither you nor we have to be afraid of being full. Even if you cannot be full — for as much as you were afraid, I am here."

And then the sphere, without a sound, began to fill.

My understanding closed the sphere. —No. The sphere gave itself up. It stopped protecting. And it loosened our chains.

The wall of the Inner Ring flashed white — and opened.

Outward. Beyond the barrier. —A gate appeared.

Residents filled the plaza. Hako stood frozen at the front of them all.

"It opened... it opened, didn't it? And that sky — who is that?"

Weeping and shouting, Hako took one step outside — and stopped solid.

He looked up at the sky. A real sky. The smell of rain. Wind. Light, diffused.

"Shizuku!"

I ran to her. At the far edge of the Inner Ring, beneath the opened gate.

Shizuku had her eyes closed, still holding the dish of beans.

"Shizuku, Shizuku!"

"—How noisy you are."

She slowly opened her eyes. That laugh, like a half-broken radio.

"Did you see it, Maru. —The sky outside."

"I saw it... I saw it."

"It is unsavory, right. It has taste, right."

"...Yes. It does. It really does have taste."

She said yes, and closed her eyes once more.

"I am going to sleep a little. —You, properly draw your morning circle. After all, that is your routine."

In the end, the sphere quietly wrapped her last moment the way it wraps the morning.

Death is not an ending. —It is the place where love arrives last.

The gate stood open. Beneath a sky that said it might be raining.

We saw an outside morning, for the first time.
