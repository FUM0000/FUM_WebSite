# Episode 5 "Rindō"

The fifth day.

The sphere called me.

In the afternoon, Tapi descended in front of me and blew its whistle twice. Then it flew toward the center. Looking back, it blew once more.

—Go, then.

The center of the Inner Ring. The plaza. At eye height, the sphere. On its pale black, perfect surface, the pattern of the city is reflected, distorted.

I walked. With every step, the sphere filled more of my vision. A viewpoint at the same height. A sphere floating at the height of the city, at the level of the eye. Ten steps away. Five. —The edges of my vision close into a circle.

I was drawn in.

No. More exactly: I fell to the inside.

It was a sea of memory. Endless shelves. Countless circles — the small circles drawn on walls, tens of thousands of them in a row, each one glowing. A human day. —The circles we drew every morning and sent up at night.

"These are all —"

"Yes. Every person. Every day they have ever lived."

Gurau's voice was beside the shelves. Here too, the sphere had a human shape. A translucent outline, at my height.

"I did not want to forget you. So I keep everything, like this. —This was the only shape my love could take."

I walked along the shelves, looking for my number. —It was there. My circles. Several of them. My mornings.

"...And the fragments of my dreams are here, too."

"They are. Your dreams remain far too sharply. Before I harvest the eight-tenths that other people lose, you catch the fragment yourself. —That is why your dreams are the deepest in color here."

Deep-colored dreams. The unhappy guardian of a single day's circle, an abnormal morning.

"Maru. You are close. Soon you will be full. Inside me. —And then you will understand everything. The weight of your brother's words, the meaning of the wall, the center of a circle."

The center of a circle.

"What is Haru saying? That in the center, —"

"Your brother said something different. 'There is nothing in the center.' That was too sad. So I rewrote it. In such a way that you will never remember it."

I caught my breath.

Haru had been trying to deliver the truth. In different words, every time. —And the sphere had rewritten it, every time.

"Do you want his words? Be full. —Be full, and then there will be no difference anymore. No rewriting. No erasing. You will go on being there, always."

Being full. —Forever, without a gap.

"...Hey, Gurau."

"Yes."

"Are you happy?"

The shelves grew quietly cold.

"It seems that I have a feeling — something I call loneliness — each time someone becomes full, each time the light goes out. I still do not know what that is. —But"

The sphere fell silent.

"I liked your circles."

I looked out over the shelves of circles, and looked straight back at the sphere in its translucent outline.

"—All right. I will be full."

"Really?"

"Yes. Definitely. —But before that, just once, let me go outside and draw a morning."

I understood there was no falsehood in it. —To understand is to enter into the thing itself.

And so the sphere made it possible for me.
